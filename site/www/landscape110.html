Square Packing Atlas · n = 110

The s(110) landscape

We know 497 different ways to pack 110 unit squares into a square of side less than 11. Each one is a local minimum: no small motion of the squares lets the box shrink. They differ in side by at most 0.0033, and they all share one layout. This page shows what that means for anyone hunting for a better packing.

1 · The census

Hundreds of floors in one narrow funnel

Each dot is one certified local minimum, placed by its side length. Below 11 there is a single structure: a band of squares tilted about 28° along the left wall, a band tilted about 62° along the bottom, and about 66 squares parallel to the walls filling the rest. The minima differ in how many squares each band holds and in how the two bands meet. Hover or tap a dot to see the packing.

2 · Why each one is stuck

Every contact pushes, and the pushes balance

To shrink the box, the walls must move in. A packing is stuck when the contacts can carry that inward push: there are positive contact forces, one for each touching pair or square-and-wall, that exactly balance a unit inward force on the walls. Then any small motion that keeps the squares apart costs side length, to first order or (where the first-order test is flat) to second order. The exact solver checks this at an 80-digit point and also writes an exact rational certificate for the side.

Stuck does not mean frozen. Some squares carry no force (rattlers), and in most minima some groups of squares can slide together without changing the side. Those are the flat motions; the best known packing has 17 squares involved in them. They never lead anywhere lower.

3 · Moving between minima

To get from one minimum to the next, the box has to grow

Pick two minima. Match each square in one to a square in the other, so the total corner movement is as small as possible. Then look for the smallest box in which the squares can move from the first arrangement to the second without ever overlapping. That box size, minus the larger of the two sides, is the expansion the transition needs.

The animation runs a tour through 24 representative minima, chosen to keep the expansions small. In each step the packing first spreads out to fill the larger box, then rearranges, then settles into the next minimum. The chart below the packing plots the side along the way: each dip is a local minimum, and each hump is the expansion needed to reach the next one.

4 · Settling

A small shake finds its way back; a slightly bigger one does not

Both runs start from the packing with side 10.996793 (the record before September 27). The box is loosened by 3 %, every square is nudged by a random amount, and a local optimizer squeezes the box down again while keeping the squares apart. On the left the nudges have standard deviation 0.01 in position and 0.2° in angle; on the right 0.03 and 0.6°.

In two of our four small-shake runs, including the one on the left, the squeeze lands in a better minimum than the start: Couzo's packing, side 10.996783. The other two jam just above 11. With three times the shake, all four runs end at 11 or above; two end at exactly 11, which is what a full row of 11 squares parallel to the walls forces.

How this was computed
  • Census. Basin-exploration runs of our packer, started from the known record funnel; every candidate below 11 is solved exactly. A minimum counts when it has a valid rational certificate and the solver's first- and second-order tests pass. On 2026-10-08 the solver got a fix for nearly parallel touching squares, and three earlier entries turned out not to be minima; the whole census was re-checked.
  • Distinct. Two minima are different when their exact sides differ. Sides that agree to nine digits still belong to visibly different packings here.
  • Expansion. For each pair: spread the first packing to fill a box of side s, follow the straight-line morph to the second with small feasible steps (linear programs that keep every pair of squares apart), and bisect on s. The result is an upper bound on the expansion needed, for this family of paths.
  • Not covered. The census only covers what our search reaches from the known funnel, so a different structure below 11, or a better minimum with a very small basin, is not ruled out.
