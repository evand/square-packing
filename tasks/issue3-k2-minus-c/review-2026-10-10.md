## 1. Statement match with our statement A / C7

* **Our definitions** (`search/FRIEDMAN.md` §0, §1.1): `c*(k) := max{c : s(k²−c) = k}`, and A ⇔ "∀c ∃k₀ ∀k≥k₀:
  s(k²−c)=k" ⇔ c*(k) → ∞.  Here s(n) is the least side of a square holding n unit squares with disjoint interiors, rotations
  allowed.  The paper uses the same s(n) (§1, line 49) and copies our c*(k) verbatim (citing FRIEDMAN.md).
* **M(k).**  FRIEDMAN.md does *not* define an M(k) for [0,k]²; our M(w) is seam capacity (§7), which is unrelated.  The
  paper's M(k) is the maximum size of a **closed** packing of [0,k]², i.e. squares pairwise disjoint as closed sets.
  Closedness is essential: with interior-disjoint squares the k×k grid gives k² and nothing can be said.
* **Reduction (Lemma 2.1): checked, correct.**  s(n) < k ⇔ n ≤ M(k).  (⇒) Dilate the centres by k/s about the common
  centre.  Interior-disjoint becomes strictly separated (u·(c₂−c₁) ≥ h₁+h₂ becomes strict), and the squares stay inside.
  (⇐) Contract by μ close to 1 using the positive minimum gap; the result fits in side μk+(1−μ)√2 < k.  Together with
  M(k) ≤ k²−1 (Cor 3.3: on a.e. line, k disjoint closed chords of length ≥ 1 cannot cover the connected segment [0,k]),
  this gives **c*(k) = k² − M(k) − 1** exactly.
* **Theorem 1.1.**  "Every closed packing of [0,k]² with N squares has k²−N ≥ κ log k for all integers k ≥ 2."  This gives
  Cor 1.2: c*(k) ≥ κ log k − 1.  Since W = k²−N is an integer, explicitly s(k²−c) = k once log k > 13.06675 + 15.21c.
  **The quantifiers match A exactly** ("for every fixed c, for all sufficiently large k"), with an effective k₀(c).  There
  is no "for infinitely many k" weakening (that would be our B).  It is a qualitative result: it says nothing below
  log k ≈ 28 (κ log k ≤ 1 there, and W ≥ 1 is trivial), and nothing new for c ≤ 4 below log k ≈ 74.
* Minor: k ≥ 2 is handled by Cor 3.3 for k < 10¹²; Sections 4–5 are used only for k ≥ 10¹².  That is consistent.

**Verdict: if the theorem holds, it proves our C7 / statement A and the site's "Unbounded" question, with no definitional
mismatch.**

## 2. Proof structure (summary)

1. Work with closed packings of [0,k]², waste W = k²−N (an integer).  Let ω(y) be the uncovered length on line y.
2. **Integrality/closedness (Lemma 3.2):** on every horizontal line at most k−1 chords have length ≥ 1.  So the short
   chords (ramps of tilted squares) plus the waste plus the excess E(y) of the long chords sum to ≥ 1 per line (Lemma 3.5).
3. **Sharpened Roth–Vaughan fundamental lemma (Prop 4.3):** for squares S₁,S₂ (or a square and the wall) with relative tilt
   θ, the waste in the RV rectangle R(S₁,S₂) is ≥ θ(2−θ)Σλ_r²/4.  Prop 4.5 improves Σλ_r² ≥ 1/(m'+1) to ≥ 0.32 for nearly
   touching, nearly parallel pairs.
4. **Multiplicity (Lemmas 4.9/4.10):** an uncovered point lies in at most 13 (analytic) or 9 (computer) rectangles R_e of
   nearly touching consecutive pairs.  With slit convexity (Lemma 4.12) and Cauchy–Schwarz this gives the cost of
   inclinations: ∫ 2β over lines ≤ A·W (Lemma 4.13), and similarly for columns (4.14), with A = 4√(K_w/Q*)/(2−ε) ≈ 10.61.
5. Lines y at distance d(y) ≥ y_min from the bottom or top side split three ways:
   (i) much waste;
   (ii) some square tilted ≥ 1/(2√2·d(y)), paid for by Lemma 4.13;
   (iii) all squares nearly axis-parallel, so the short chords come from ramps of nearly axis-parallel squares Z.
6. **Quantization (Lemma 5.2):** if the column under Z down to the bottom side is "rigid" (vertical waste < 10⁻⁵, tilts
   < 1/(2s)), then Z's ramps sit within 1.265·10⁻⁵ of an integer.  Lines with fractional part in [w₀, 1−w₀] therefore force
   non-rigid columns, except on crack sets of measure < ½ per square (Lemmas 5.3/5.4).
7. **Non-rigid columns cost waste** (Lemma 4.14 plus vertical waste).  Charge each column at its least failing scale and
   integrate over scales with Tonelli.  Integer k makes the same work from the top side.
8. Integrating over lines with weight dy/d(y) over y_min ≤ d(y) ≤ k/2 gives ℓ(H) ≈ 2 log k ≤ 30.418·W/(1−ω₀)h₀.
   So W ≥ 1.99954(log k − 13.06675)/30.418.

It is in the Roth–Vaughan style (waste along lines, RV rectangles), but with two new ingredients: the integrality/closedness
count k−1, and quantization measured from both walls.

### Lemma 4.10 (K_w* = 9, computer-assisted): **the reduction looks right and the code looks sound as read; independent
re-execution of the box verification is still outstanding**

*Reduction (paper, lines 750–783).*  Let I be the set of all centres within ρ₁ of p, so n ≤ 5.  Then

  #relevant edges ≤ U(I) = E_II + W_I + Σ_i D_i*(I),

where
* E_II counts pairs in I at distance < Λ;
* W_I counts centres that can have a wall edge (at most one each);
* D_i* is the maximum number of γ₀-separated directions of outer partners x with |x| > ρ₁, 1 ≤ |x−c_i| < Λ,
  p ∈ R(c_i, x), and |x−c_j| ≥ 1 for j ≠ i.

I checked that every relevant edge is counted at least once, and that the partner-direction separation γ₀ is valid.  The
"all wall rectangles stand on one side" argument is also right: for adjacent sides the two centres would have
|c−c'|² ≤ ½ < 1.  Ignoring the other sides only drops constraints.  The no-wall run normalizes θ₁ = 0 (a rotation); the
wall run fixes the frame by the side y = −h with h ∈ (0, d+√2/2].  Wall-run boxes with W = 0 ("nowall") are correctly
deferred to the no-wall run, whose constraint set is a subset.

*Code vs lemma (read, not run).*
* **Conditions.**  `certified_violation` (Arb, 96 bits) tests exactly:
  * |x|² < ρ₁²,
  * t = −r cos(φ−θ) < 0 or t > ρ, or |r sin(φ−θ)| > ½ (that is, p ∉ R(c_i, x)),
  * |x−c_j|² < 1,
  * x_y < −h+½.
  Each is tested as a certain violation over the whole (φ-cell × ρ-interval × box), with two levels of subdivision.  The
  float prefilter only *proposes* discards, and each discard is certified in Arb, so it is sound.
* **E, W and infeasibility** are recomputed in Arb with the exact constants (`LAM`, `RHO1SQ`, `WALL_R` as arb).  Every
  leaf, including the ones labelled 'inf' or 'nowall', goes through check_inf → check_nowall → rig_bound2, with
  sub-bisection, so the float labels are not trusted.
* **Domain.**
  * The float constants are upper bounds.  I checked this independently: LAM, RHO1 and WALL_R exceed the exact values by
    9e-17, 7e-17 and 4e-17.  The float G0 is 1.3e-16 below exact γ₀ (conservative), and the code subtracts a further 1e-12.
  * `np.linspace(1, LAM, 7)[-1] == LAM`.
  * The initial θ slices use m ∈ {4, 8, 16}, so 2π_f·m/m == 2π_f exactly.  The extension in `iv2` (triggered on
    hi == 2π_f) therefore fires for every interval it needs to.
  * The cell end points are fixed by PHI_HI_EFF = max(PHI_HI[k], PHI_LO[k+1]).  This fixes the one-ulp gaps that
    invalidated the first verifier (`SUPERSEDED.md`).
* **`max_points`** takes the greedy count from each run start, with separation γ₀−10⁻¹² and the closing condition relaxed
  by 10⁻¹².  It is a valid upper bound on the maximum number of γ₀-separated points in the closed arcs.  The
  rotate-back exchange argument is correct, and a 10⁻¹⁵ discrepancy between certified and float arcs is absorbed by the
  10⁻¹² margin.  It runs in floating point.  The paper's argument that the comparisons are never near ties is plausible:
  j·γ₀ against multiples of π/720 differ by > 4·10⁻⁵.

## 4. Relation to the literature

* **Roth–Vaughan (1978).**  W(α) ≫ √(α‖α‖) when α(α−[α]) > 1/6.  For α → k⁻ this tends to 0, so it gives nothing at
  integer sides; our FRIEDMAN.md §0 says the same.  The new paper's integer-specific inputs, the closedness count k−1 and
  the quantization from both walls, are exactly what RV lacks.
* **Bui 2504.09489** ("only good squares matter") and the upper bounds (Erdős–Graham, Chung–Graham 2009/2020, Bui
  2508.04603, McClenagan 2602.01484) say nothing about lower bounds at integer k.  Chung–Graham 2009 only *expect* s(k²−c)=k
  for fixed c and large k.
* **Our records.**  `notes/wishlist-literature-2026-10-03.md` §6 says "c*(k) → ∞ is open, and no published source claims
  it or attacks it".  FRIEDMAN.md C7 says "open in the literature".  The site's "Unbounded" item says "we found no attack
  on it in print".  I know of no prior proof.  If correct, this would be the first proof of A, though log-rate and with
  useless constants for concrete k.
* **Discrepancy to fix on our side.**  FRIEDMAN.md §0 and the wishlist quote RV as W ≥ 10⁻¹⁰⁰√(α‖α‖) ([S], secondhand).
  The preprint, whose reviewers checked the original, says RV's fundamental lemma has c = 10⁻¹⁰ (p. 171, (3)) and states the
  theorem with ≫.  Worth checking against the original before we quote either number.
