Twelve and Thirteen Squares

Nobody knows whether twelve unit squares fit inside a square of side less than 4. The best proven barrier had stood since 2003, at 3.7889. Here is a new one at 3.9686, machine-checked from end to end — closing about 85% of the remaining gap — with a new floor for eleven squares, a case-free proof that thirteen need side 4, and an honest account of how far the method does not go.

01 · The result

A new lower bound for s(12)

Theorem. Twelve unit squares cannot be packed into any square of side smaller than 15680/3951. That is, s(12) ≥ 3.968616….

3.788854  →  3.968616

Previous published bound: 2 + 4/√5 = 3.788854… (Stromquist 2003, inherited from n = 11 by monotonicity, and listed as the record for n = 11–12 in Friedman's dynamic survey DS7). The conjectured truth is s(12) = 4.

The proof is a weighted unavoidable set: a finite set of points carrying weights, such that every unit square inside the container — at every position and every angle — captures total weight at least 1. Twelve squares with disjoint interiors would then capture at least 12, so a certificate weighing less than 12 rules them out.

The certificate has 1736 points with rational coordinates of denominator 3951, in a container of side 15680/3951, and total weight 11.9738036 — under the threshold by 0.026. A 764-point certificate proves s(12) ≥ 980/247 = 3.967611, and the first bound published here, 3920/997 = 3.931795, now needs only 224 points. The same pipeline gives s(11) ≥ 3040/797 = 3.814304 (680 points).

A weaker certificate is far prettier. Insist that every point carry the same weight, and the best one found is:

81 points in [0, 35/9]² such that every closed unit square inside that container, at every angle, contains at least 7 of them.

Twelve squares with disjoint interiors would need 12 × 7 = 84 point-slots and only 81 points exist, so at most ⌊81/7⌋ = 11 fit — hence s(12) ≥ 35/9 = 3.888…, already past the 2003 bound.

02 · Context

Where the problem stands

Let s(n) be the side of the smallest square holding n unit squares, rotations allowed. It is known exactly for surprisingly few n.

ns(n)status
103.707107…proved — Stromquist 2003
113.877084…best packing (Trump 1979); open — bound raised here to 3.8143
124  (conjectured)open — bound raised here to 3.9686
134proved — Bentz 2010; re-proved here without cases (§07)
14, 15, 164proved — El Moumni, Nagamochi, area

Because s(13) = 4 is proved and s(11) < 4, the largest n with s(n) < 4 is either 11 or 12 — and deciding which is the open problem. The gap for n = 12 was [3.788854, 4]; it is now [3.9686, 4].

03 · The other direction

No packing below 4 was found

A basin-hopping search over all 36 coordinates, validated on n = 5, 10 and 11, finds nothing below side 4 — every run collapses to the compressed 4 × 4 grid. Schadt's and Ellsworth's 2025–26 annealing campaigns, which improved many records, never improved 12.

04 · The certificate

What the 81 points look like

A linear program chooses the points and their fractional weights, where the classical proofs (Göbel, Stromquist, Friedman, Nagamochi, Bentz) hand-design them. The bound was sharpened in three steps: scaling the first certificate up to its critical container (3.92 → 3.9318), re-optimising the weights with the exact verifier supplying violated placements as cutting planes (3.9486), and growing the point set by column generation (3.9686); at 3.9696 column generation no longer gets under 12. The uniform certificates come from an integer program that forces equal weights — 1/7 each for the 81-point set.

05 · Verification

How it is checked

A bound like this is worth what its verification is worth, so the covering property is checked three independent ways.

Lean 4 + Mathlib

The reduction step — an unavoidable set of total weight W implies at most W squares — is fully formalised, including the scaling argument that lets a closed-square certificate control a packing. 0 sorries; axioms are only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

Exact integer verifier (Rust)

Checks the covering property over the entire continuum of placements. Angles are enumerated as rational rotations θ_k = 2·arctan(k/N), so all trigonometry is rational; for each bin, a unit square at any angle in the bin contains the concentric square of side σ_k = 1/(cos δ + sin δ) at angle θ_k. For each such angle the minimum over the continuum of centres is computed exactly by an arrangement sweep — no sampling. All arithmetic in i128.

Two independent re-checks

A separately written Python implementation in exact Fraction arithmetic checks every angle bin exhaustively and agrees bin by bin on every certificate. A third check — a dense float scan over 181 angles spanning the full 0°–90° range, ~500k centres each, not using the symmetry reduction — returns the same minimum for the original 788-point certificate, 1.0000023, as the exact checks do.

Resolution robustness

The main certificate is verified at N = 6000 and 12000; on coarser nets the angle-bin shrink exceeds its slack and the verifier refuses it. The uniform certificates are verified at N = 2000 and 8000. The D4 symmetry of the point set — itself checked — reduces angles to [0°, 45°].

06 · Limits

What this does not do

It does not prove s(12) = 4, and this method never will. By LP duality a certificate at container side s weighs at least the fractional packing number ν_f(s), and an exact fractional packing of mass 12.0282 at s = 3.99 shows that no weighted cover below 12 exists at any side ≥ 3.99, whatever points or verifier are used (DUAL_EXACT.md). So the ceiling of this whole family of arguments lies in [3.968616, 3.99). It is not 4.

The container itself does not help: a cover of [0,4]² — in the closed convention, where a point on a square's edge counts, explained in §07 — weighs at least 12.2688 (COVER4.md). Case analysis layered on top, Bentz's route for thirteen, has been measured there too, and every branching tried leaves a case still worth 12 (BENTZ.md). The missing unit is rotation — mostly by less than one degree — and on the LP's extremal measure it is carried by eight squares at once, a statement no weighting of points can express. That is a real research problem, not a longer run.

The complete negative record — every route to s(12) = 4 tried, where each one fails, each claim labelled proved or measured — is notes/n12-gap.md.

07 · A second result

s(13) = 4, without case analysis

The same machinery gives a new proof of a known theorem. That thirteen unit squares need side 4 is Bentz's 2010 result, from a point set plus a six-case analysis of where a square may sit. Here it follows from a single object, with no cases.

Theorem. There are 3621 points in the square [0,4]², carrying rational weights of total 12.955972155, such that every closed unit square inside [0,4]² — at every position and every angle — captures weight at least 1.

12.955972  <  13

Thirteen squares packed below side 4 would rescale to thirteen pairwise disjoint closed unit squares in [0,4]², capturing 13 between them without double-counting a single point. They cannot: the whole set weighs less than 13. And sixteen unit squares tile [0,4]², so 4 is attained — s(13) = 4. Certificate: certificates/rung2/s13_closed_cover_4.txt.

What a “closed cover” is, and why weight under 13 is enough

A cover is the certificate of §01 placed in the container of side 4 itself. Closed means a point exactly on a square's edge counts as captured — the limit of the 1 + ε squares every published proof argues about, and what makes 13 reachable. With open squares the sixteen tiles of the 4×4 grid, shrunk a hair, are disjoint, so every cover would weigh at least 16. Closed, they share their boundary points, and 12.96 is possible — against a proved floor of 12.2688, which is all the room this proof has.

“Margin zero” and “exhaustive”

The verifier of §05 shrinks each square slightly so that one square stands in for a bin of angles. At the container that cannot work: at the corner placement the only captured point lies on the square's edge, so the slack is zero, and a tilt of ε buys back only about ε² while a bin of width ε erodes about ε.

Instead the three-dimensional space of placements is cut adaptively into boxes with rational corners, and for each box the checker must prove, exactly, that every placement in it captures weight at least 1, splitting the box when it cannot. Exhaustive means it finished with zero boxes uncertified; nothing is sampled, and no floating-point number is load-bearing. Boxes certified by a fixed set of captured points can only prove covers of weight 16 or more (RUNG2.md), so most boxes are certified disjunctively — “either this set of points is inside, or that one” — 65% of the non-empty boxes in both checkers.

The two were written from the statement, not from each other: different subdivisions, different primitives, no shared code. They also agree pointwise — at four specific placements, one with a point exactly on the edge, they return the same nine-digit rationals. Twenty-three rejection tests check that the Rust checker refuses mutated certificates, two invalid covers from our own history, and nine malformed files (on which it must give no verdict at all).

The soundness of every primitive both checkers rest on is proved in Lean 4 + Mathlib (lean/Sqpack/ZeroMargin.lean, 0 sorries, standard axioms only). Not in Lean, in either checker: the subdivision, the argument that the boxes exhaust the space, the bookkeeping and the parser. Behind those stands the agreement of two independent programs.
