# k2m3-lit — prior status of s(k²−3) = k (2026-09-30; report returned as text, saved by coordinator)

1. Bentz never says "conjecture". arXiv:1606.03746v1 abstract: "Together with the result s(6)=3 by Keaney [sic] and Shiu,
   these results strongly suggest that s(m²−3)=m for m ≥ 3". EJC 2010 only: "strongly suggest ... m = 5, 6".
   Older + stronger: Friedman's survey DS7, Conjecture 1 (1998 through 2009): "If s(n²−k)=n, then s((n+1)²−k)=n+1";
   with s(6)=3 it implies s(m²−3)=m for all m ≥ 3.  Neither Bentz paper mentions it.
2. k=3: Kearney & Shiu, EJC 9 (2002) #R14 (also s(7)=3).  k=4,7: Bentz, EJC 17 (2010) #R126 (refereed).
   k=5,6: Bentz arXiv:1606.03746 (12 Jun 2016), v1 only, no journal ref found (absence of evidence).  Ellsworth's page
   says "Proved by Wolfram Bentz in October 2018" (likely the PDF render date).  k ≥ 8: open everywhere checked.
3. Nagamochi EJC 12 (2005) #R37: s(k²−2)=s(k²−1)=k; previous s(22) ≥ √15+1, s(33) ≥ √24+1.  Friedman survey latest
   14 Aug 2009.  Ellsworth "Squares in Squares" (updated Sep 2026): 6, 13, 22, 33, 46 proved; nothing for 61, 78.
4. No 2025–26 claims found for the statement, s(61)=8 or s(78)=9.  jlevy/squares H-033 (2026-08-24, open): "Can
   Bentz's method prove s(61) = 8?"; jlevy audited Bentz 2010 (Lemma 10 transposed point, Theorem 8 holds) and replayed
   Bentz 2016 Thm 11 (24/24).  Mentions n=61 bound ≈ 7.8906 (Bašić–Slivková, unverified).  X not directly searchable.

Suggested sentence: "Friedman [survey, 1998] conjectured that s(n²−k)=n implies s((n+1)²−k)=n+1, which together with
s(6)=3 [Kearney–Shiu 2002] would give s(k²−3)=k for all k ≥ 3. Bentz, having proved s(13)=4 and s(46)=7 [Bentz 2010],
stated explicitly that s(m²−3)=m should hold for all m ≥ 3 and proved the cases m=5,6 in an unpublished preprint
[Bentz 2016]. The cases k ≥ 8 were previously open; we prove s(k²−3)=k for all k ≥ 6."
