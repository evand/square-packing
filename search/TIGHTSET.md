## Reading

**The algebra.**  With `m = m(t)` the exact minimum captured weight and `W` the total, `w/m` is a cover of
total `W/m = 12 + E`.  A packing of 12 unit squares in a container of side `t' < t`, scaled up to
side `t`, gives 12 closed unit squares with pairwise disjoint closures, each capturing `c_i m` with
`c_i >= 1`, and `sum_i c_i m <= W` because no point is counted twice.  So `sum_i (c_i - 1) <= W/m - 12 = E`,
every term is non-negative, and each square individually satisfies `captured <= m(1+E) = W - 11m`.
In numerator units `W - 11m` is an integer, so the threshold needs no rounding.  The verifier's cell
sums are those of the `sigma_k`-shrunk square at `theta_k`; a unit square at any angle of the bin contains
that shrunk square and captures at least as much, so the set of cells with `sum <= W - 11m` is a
**superset** of the true near-tight set -- the safe direction for a case analysis.  (The verifier's
`m` is likewise a lower bound on the true minimum, so `E` is an upper bound on the true excess.)

**The discontinuity at the critical side.**  `m(t)` does not decrease smoothly: at `t = 3.9687`,
only `8e-5` above the critical `15680/3951 = 3.968616`, it has already dropped from `1.0000056` to
`0.9867834` (a step of `0.0132`), and it stays at exactly that value through `3.9690`
(`m(3.9688)` is the same; `runs/tight_3.9688_min.txt`), then `0.98498` at `3.9692`, `0.97105` at
`3.9695`, `0.96191` at `3.970`, and collapses to `0.690` at `3.972` and `0.220` at `3.990`.  The
reason is the closed-square convention plus the LP: the optimiser puts atoms *exactly* on the
boundary of the tight squares, so the moment the picture is scaled by any `lambda > 1` those atoms
leave and the minimum jumps.  Consequently the smallest excess this certificate can offer beyond its
critical side is `E = 0.134` (threshold `1.119`), not something tending to `0`.

**Growth with t.**  The near-tight set is not small for any `t` above critical:

* exactly tight at critical (`threshold = m`, the reference set): `0.35%` of pose space, `739,560` cells;
* `t = 3.9687`, `3.9690` (`E = 0.134`): `18.2%`, `1.49e9` cells;
* `t = 3.9695` (`E = 0.33`): `55.5%`;  `t = 3.970` (`E = 0.45`): `74.2%`;
* `t >= 3.972` (`E > 5`): `100.0%` -- the threshold `4.38+` exceeds what any unit square captures, and
  the count equals the whole arrangement (`7.17e9` cells at `3.990`).

So the requested range `3.970 ... 3.990` is entirely in the regime where the constraint
`captured <= W - 11m` is vacuous or nearly so; the only informative values are within about `0.001`
of the critical side, and even there the set is `18%` of pose space.

**Structure of the sets.**

* At the critical size the tight set is one `C4` orbit of *corner* poses: centres within
  `0.5 <= x, y <= 0.75` of a corner (98.5% of the measure in the corner boxes; distance from the wall
  `0.5 - 0.67`), at **all** angles from `0` to `45 deg` but with the measure decreasing roughly linearly
  from `12.8%` per degree at `0 - 1 deg` to `0.4%` per degree beyond `14 deg` (a corner square can rotate
  and still hit the same atoms).  Nothing in the interior is tight.  This is highly structured:
  4 clusters, all equal by symmetry, one up to symmetry.
* At `E = 0.134` (`t = 3.9687`) the set has two connected pieces (`runs/tight_3.9687.png`):
  a **frame** -- centres at distance `0.5 - 0.83` from the walls, the whole way round, `46%` of the
  measure, corners still the densest part -- and a central **pinwheel**: four bent lobes around the
  container centre at distance `1.16 - 1.82` from the nearest wall (`48%`), rotated by roughly
  `+-30 deg` from the axes in the spirit of a pinwheel packing, with a hole at the exact centre.  The
  band at wall distance `0.85 - 1.15` is empty.  All angle bins are present; the angle histogram is
  almost flat (`4.2%` per degree at `0 deg` falling to `1.7%` per degree at `20 - 45 deg`; mean `18.9 deg`).
  So the near-tight set has a recognisable geometry (frame + pinwheel, `2` clusters) but it is far from
  "few isolated poses": each cluster is a continuum of centres over the full angular range.
* By `E = 0.33` (`t = 3.9695`) the frame and pinwheel merge into one cluster (`55%` of pose space)
  and the wall-distance profile fills in; at `t = 3.970` only four roundish holes around the
  `2x2` "second-row" positions and a small hole at the centre remain (`runs/tight_3.970.png`).
  The angle distribution is essentially uniform from here on.

**Reading for a case analysis.**  The near-tight-set argument with *this* certificate rescaled
does not isolate a small set of poses: the LP-degenerate tight set at the critical side already
has positive measure (`0.35%` of pose space, an entire corner orbit at every angle), and the
first admissible `E` beyond the critical side is `0.134`, at which point `18%` of pose space is
admissible for every square.  Two consequences:

1. Rescaling a critical certificate is the wrong way to obtain a near-tight cover at `t`: the
   discontinuity means `E(t) >= 0.134` no matter how close `t` is to critical.  A cover for
   `t` should be re-optimised *at* `t` (the `tighten.py` machinery with the verifier as separation
   oracle at container `t`), which would bring `E(t)` down continuously towards its LP-optimal value
   `nu_f(t)/1 - 12` -- the ceiling analysis in `search/CEILING.md` suggests that value is small but
   positive only up to `t ~ 3.97`.
2. Even a small `E` will not make the set tiny: the tight set at critical is a corner orbit at all
   angles, so a case analysis would have to be a *frame + interior* argument -- e.g. "every square
   has its centre either within `0.83` of a wall or in the central pinwheel region", combined with a
   counting argument on how many squares the frame can hold -- rather than an enumeration of
   isolated candidate poses.  The measure maps in `runs/tight_*.png` give the regions to use.

**Caveats.**  The measure is computed from the verifier's cells at `N = 6000` (`2486` bins over
`[0, 45 deg]`), so it is the measure of the superset described above, not of the true near-tight
set; the difference is the shrink `sigma_k` (about `2e-4` in side) and is negligible at this
resolution.  Cell areas are clipped to the rotated admissible centre box in `f64` (the sampled cells
are re-clipped independently in Python and agree to `1e-12`).  Cluster counts are on a
`48 x 48 x 0.5 deg` voxel grid with 26-connectivity and are therefore "connected up to a gap of
one voxel (`0.083`)"; the plots show `~3e5` uniformly sampled cells per `t`, while the measure maps
and all numbers use every cell.  The `_cells.txt` dumps in `runs/` are `47 - 126 MB` each and hold
the bin statistics, the histograms and the cell samples.
