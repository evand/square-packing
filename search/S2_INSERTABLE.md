# S2: insertable box covers at the threshold (c = 3, k = 4; c = 4, k = 5) — 2026-10-03

Brief: `tasks/s2-insertable/README.md`.  Background: `FRIEDMAN.md` §2.1 (insertion lemma), §8 (row lemma), §9.1, §9.4 (S2).
Code: `search/s2_insertable.py`.  Runs: `runs/s2_*` (ignored).  Labels: **[proved]**, **[measured]** (float LP over
sampled rows plus float scans, no bound either way), **[heuristic]**.  Nothing here is certified.

## 0. Answer

> **No-go at both thresholds [measured].**  A valid closed cover that is insertable in the full sense (q ≤ 1, so the
> saving carries to every k′ ≥ k) costs far more than the unrestricted LP on the same grid:
>
> | k | c | baseline LP (saving) | insertable, q ≤ 1, class (b) (saving) | price | needed |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 4 | 3 | 12.3254 (3.675) | **14.1925 (1.808)** | **1.87** | saving > 3 |
> | 5 | 4 | 20.7489 (4.251) [LINE_COVER, same q = 50 grid] | **22.4745 (2.526)** (not fully converged, see §3) | **≈ 1.73** | saving > 4 |
>
> In class (a) (points and segments only) the full insertable LP is **infeasible, and provably so** (§2, Lemma 1): an
> insertable certificate with q ≤ 1 must be exactly Lebesgue on the slab crossing square.  So **S2 fails at c = 3 (k = 4)
> and at c = 4 (k = 5)** for both measure classes on this grid, by a margin (1.2 and 1.5 below the needed saving) that is
> two orders of magnitude larger than any grid or row-sampling effect.  **No exact certificate run is warranted.**
>
> The q ≤ 1 constraint is what kills it.  Dropping it (the "one-step" LP, which keeps only p_x + p_y + q ≤ 2k+1)
> costs only **0.30 (k = 4)** and **0.21 (k = 5)**.  Such a cover still proves a **finite** ladder of k′ (§4): k′ = 4..8
> for c = 3 and k′ = 5..8 for c = 4 (floats; the k = 5 margin is only 0.04).

## 1. Setup

* **Slab.**  t = (k−1−√2)/2: k = 4 → 0.792893, slab [0.7929, 3.2071]; k = 5 → 1.292893, slab [1.2929, 3.7071].
* **Atoms** (identical in every LP of one k): points on the 1/1000 grid, from the 0.05 lattice, the support of the
  LINE_COVER finals (`lc_m4germ_r21`, `lc_m5germC_r12`) and dual pricing on the 0.01 grid.  There are no points on grid
  lines or walls (as `line_cover --no-line-points`).  Segment pieces on the interior lines x, y ∈ {1..k−1} use the
  1/50 grid refined at the six breakpoints {t, t+√2−1, t+1, t+√2, t+2, t+1+√2}.  That set is closed under the ±1
  shifts inside the slab and under D4, so the restriction of μ to the slab is well defined piecewise.  Class (b)
  adds Lebesgue squares [a, k−a]² with a ∈ {0, 0.1, …} ∪ {t}, all a ≤ t, density = column value.  Any closed axis
  rectangle that contains the slab cross-section is periodic in both slabs.
* **Baseline** = D4-orbit columns (closed semantics, `line_cover` coefficients).  **Insertable** = the same LP with
  columns aggregated into orbits of the group generated by D4 and the slab shifts (x for atoms in the vertical slab,
  y in the horizontal one).  This is exactly "μ restricted to each slab is 1-periodic", with equal values on each class.
  D4 is WLOG: the D4 average of an insertable valid μ is insertable and valid (the slabs are centred).
* **Mass rows:** `sum` = p_x + p_y + q ≤ 2k+1; `full` = sum and q ≤ 1 (half-open strips [t, t+1)).
* **Rows:** warm rows from the LINE_COVER finals and their dip files, the germ family at q = 50 (permanent), and
  `line_cover` phase-A cutting planes per round (0.01 lattice at 1° steps with a random shift, polish, near-tile and
  item-3 families).  LP: HiGHS IPM.

## 2. Bookkeeping, and the lemma that decides class (a)

**Bookkeeping [proved, and checked in floats].**  μ′ = μ|_{x<t+1} + τ₁(μ|_{x≥t}) has disjoint pieces, so the
horizontal row mass becomes p_y + q.  The two insertions therefore add p_x + p_y + q, and the new period masses are
p_x + q, p_y + q, q.  At step j the box gains p_x + p_y + (2j+1)q against a budget of 2(k+j)+1, so the saving is
preserved at every step iff p_x + p_y + q ≤ 2k+1 and q ≤ 1.  Checks (`s2_insertable.py bookkeep`, `runs/s2_bookkeep.log`):

| measure | p_x = p_y | q | p_x+p_y+q | 2k+1 | total after x+y insertion | float scan, box k / inserted k+1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lebesgue [0,k]², k = 4, 5, 7 | k | 1 | 2k+1 | 2k+1 | (k+1)² | — |
| `qx2_data/L4_k02_box7.txt` (k = 7) | 7.000000 | 1.000000 | 15.000000 | 15 | 45.611030 + 15 = 60.611030 (saving 3.388970 kept) | 1.000000 / 1.000000 |

The box7 periodicity defect is 1e-15.  On the inserted box the centred slab t′ = t + ½ gives p_x′ = p_x + q = 8 as
predicted.  The same insertion run on the LP's insertable k = 4 cover (§3) gives total + 9.000000 and min 1.000000 on
both boxes.

**Lemma 1 (crossing square is Lebesgue) [proved].**  Let μ be valid on [0,k]² and insertable with slab
[t, t+1+√2] in both directions, and let q = μ([t,t+1)²).  Then q ≥ 1, and if q ≤ 1 then μ restricted to the crossing
square Q = [t, t+1+√2]² is Lebesgue measure.

*Proof.*  On Q, μ is 1-periodic in both directions.  Let μ̃ be its periodic extension to ℝ², which is well defined
because Q is wider than 1.  A unit square S at angle θ has bounding width ≤ √2.  So S + v ⊂ Q for every v in a full
period cell, since Q leaves 1+√2−√2 ≥ 1 of room.  Averaging over the cell gives ∫ μ̃(S+v) dv = q·|S| = q, and validity
gives μ̃(S+v) ≥ 1, so q ≥ 1.  If q = 1 then μ̃(S+v) = 1 for a.e. v and every θ.  In Fourier terms
μ̂(ξ)·1̂_{S_θ}(ξ) = 0 for every ξ ∈ 2πℤ² \ {0} and every θ.  For fixed ξ ≠ 0, 1̂_{S_θ}(ξ) = sinc(⟨ξ,e_θ⟩/2)·
sinc(⟨ξ,e_θ^⊥⟩/2) is nonzero for generic θ.  Hence μ̂(ξ) = 0 for ξ ≠ 0, and μ̃ is Lebesgue.  ∎

This is the mechanism of FRIEDMAN §8, row lemma (iii) ("no seam without drift"), applied in two directions at once.
Consequences:
* **Class (a) cannot be fully insertable.**  Points and segments are never Lebesgue, so q > 1 strictly.  Every class-(a)
  insertable cover leaks 2(q−1) of saving per step, and the ladder it proves is finite (§4).
* **Class (b):** the crossing square must carry exactly density 1 and no atoms.  The LP therefore drops all atoms in Q
  (`noX`).  That is an exact reduction, not a restriction.
* Together with the waste bound (Chung–Graham, O(k^{3/5})), q = 1 also forces p_x + p_y = 2k.  Otherwise the saving
  would grow linearly in the number of insertions.  Every full LP below indeed has p_x = p_y = k.000 and q = 1.000.
* Measured confirmation.  At k = 4, the class-(a) `full` LP on the final rows reports "optimal" 13.3126 with q = 1.0000.
  That is a row-sampling artefact: a float scan of that cover finds **min 0.7648** (`runs/s2_bookkeep.log`).  On the
  same rows, the class-(b) LP with Q's atoms allowed (13.3126, same cover) is likewise invalid.  With them dropped it
  gives 14.1925 at min 1.000000.  At low resolution (smoke test) HiGHS reports the class-(a) `full` LP **Infeasible**.

* **Price of insertability L(k) − S_ins(k):** 1.87 (k = 4) and ≈ 1.73 (k = 5) for the full version, 0.30 and 0.21 for
  one-step.
* **(a) vs (b).**  Lebesgue mass is worth nothing to the baseline or to the one-step LPs (identical values), and it is
  everything for the full version, where Lemma 1 makes it mandatory.  For the Lean side: polygon (Lebesgue) mass is
  *necessary* for any insertable certificate that is meant to propagate to all k, and useless otherwise.
* **Where the Lebesgue square sits.**  At k = 4 the LP uses a single square [0.7, 3.3]² at density 1, just covering the
  crossing square [0.793, 3.207]².  It is not pushed into the wall ring.  The full LP's k = 4 cover has points 2.01,
  segments 5.42, Lebesgue 6.76.  At k = 4 the crossing square overlaps the unit wall band on [0.79, 1].
* **k = 5 convergence.**  The full (b) LP was stopped after 2 rounds.  Between rounds its value moved by +0.011 while
  the residual stayed at 0.93–0.96: the pruning cycle was dropping rows.  A sampled-row LP over a fixed column set
  is a relaxation, so more rows can only raise the value.  The number 22.47 is therefore an *upper* bound on the
  saving of an insertable cover on this grid, up to grid refinement (≲ 0.03 by the LINE_COVER q/rows experience).
  The verdict does not depend on it.
* **Baselines on identical rows.**  The plan was to re-solve every variant on one common final row set.  That broke
  down because pruning was keyed to the insertable solutions.  A baseline re-solved on the pruned k = 4 rows gave
  12.263 at resid 0.77, so it is not valid and is not used.  The baselines quoted are converged loops on the same atom
  grid and row recipe.  The gaps they are compared against are ≥ 0.2 (one-step) and ≥ 1.7 (full).

## 4. What the q > 1 (one-step) covers still prove [measured]

The saving after J insertion steps is S + Σ_{j<J} (s₀ − 2j(q−1)), with s₀ = 2k+1 − (p_x+p_y+q):

| box | S | s₀ | q − 1 | saving at k′ = k, k+1, … | last k′ with saving > c |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k = 4, c = 3 | 3.3757 | 0.3376 | 0.1167 | 3.376, 3.713, 3.818, 3.688, 3.326, 2.730 | **k′ = 8** (from 4) |
| k = 5, c = 4 | 4.0380 | 0.2447 | 0.0995 | 4.038, 4.283, 4.328, 4.175, 3.823 | **k′ = 8** (from 5) |

Both are float LP values, not certificates.  The c = 3 ladder (k′ = 4..8, smallest margin 0.33 at k′ = 8) is a cheap
side result.  It would be a one-box proof of s(k²−3) = k for k = 4..8 if certified.  But F₃ is already a theorem, so
it is only of methodological interest (a case-free "one box → five values" certificate).  The c = 4 ladder starts
at margin 0.04, which is too thin for an exact run, and F₄ already holds.  These covers were optimised for total
mass, not ladder length.  An LP that maximises the ladder (e.g. adding Σ_{j<J} constraints for a target J) would do
better [heuristic].

## 5. Reading, and next step

* **[measured] S2 is false at c = 3 and c = 4 on this grid, by a wide margin.**  k_ins(3) > 4 and k_ins(4) > 5.
  (FRIEDMAN 9.1.3 had k_ins(4) ∈ [5, 8]; now [6, 8].)
* **[heuristic] Why the price is ≈ 1.7–1.9 and roughly k-independent.**  Lemma 1 plus p_x = p_y = k (forced) mean the
  slab column [t, t+1) × [0,k] carries exactly k: zero net saving in each slab column.  So the walls inside the two
  slabs, a length 1+√2 on each of the 4 sides, cannot keep their deficit unless the slab interior pays it back, and
  the interior is pinned to Lebesgue on Q.  The baseline wall ring saves ≈ 0.27 per unit length (S60_COVER §4).
  The splice region removes about 4 × 2.414 × 0.27 ≈ 2.6 of it, offset partly by the corners/edges being re-optimised.
  The measured 1.73–1.87 is consistent with **S1 (bounded splice cost) with 2δ ≈ 1.7–1.9**, i.e. δ ≈ 0.9 per direction.
  If δ stays there, the first k where an insertable LP clears c = 4 needs L(k) ≳ 5.8.  L(7) ≈ 5.21 and L(8) ≈ 5.60
  (S60_COVER §4), so **k_ins(4) ≈ 8–9, about k_fam(4) = 8**: no gain over the R = w = 3 family for c = 4.
  This contradicts nothing in 9.1: "seam capacity is the price of insertability", and here it is paid at full rate.
* **Recommendation.**  Do **not** start an exact certificate at k = 4 or 5 (either class).  If S1 is to be measured,
  the cheap next test is the full (b) insertable LP at k = 6 and k = 7 (empirical δ(k)).  The S1 prediction is a saving
  of ≈ L(k) − 1.8: ≈ 2.5 at k = 6, ≈ 3.4 at k = 7.  A clearly smaller price at k = 7 would reopen S2-type shortcuts for
  c = 5; k_fam(5) ≤ 10.  Budget ≈ 1–2·10⁴ CPU-s, dominated by single-threaded HiGHS solves; run variants as parallel
  processes.  The one-step ladder (§4) is the only positive by-product.
